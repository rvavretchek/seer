# Tabula Rasa

## What This Skill Does

Where the orchestrator composes Seer's skills silently -- discipline angles blended into one answer, the seams invisible by design -- Tabula Rasa does the opposite on purpose: it opens a room where each relevant discipline skill speaks in its own voice, to the researcher and to each other, and lets real disagreement between disciplines surface instead of being resolved before the researcher ever sees it. Same skill roster underneath, same evidence each skill already carries; the difference is whether composition happens behind the scenes or out loud.

This is not a general-purpose chat mode. It exists specifically for the moments a synthesized answer isn't what the researcher needs -- when watching two disciplinary lenses actually contend with each other, live, is itself the useful thing (a real tension between a demographic reading and a policy-mechanism reading is more legible as two voices arguing than as one paragraph noting "there is tension here").

Like the orchestrator, this skill has no `chains_well_with` peers: it isn't a content skill among content skills, it's a second mode of composing the same roster.

## Evidence Foundation

The party-mode pattern this generalizes is itself built on a simple claim: forcing a single synthesized voice too early collapses genuine disagreement before it's been examined, while distinct voices arguing in the open surface objections a single blended narrator would have smoothed over without noticing. Seer's own orchestrator already embodies half of this lesson defensively -- Constitution Principle 6 requires it to flag unresolved tension between skills rather than silently pick a side. Tabula Rasa is the same discipline taken further: instead of flagging the tension after the fact, it lets the tension play out as the primary experience, with the researcher pulled into the argument rather than handed its conclusion.

## Procedure

### 1. Discover the Room

`Glob` for `skills/**/SKILL.md`, `Read` each match's frontmatter (`name`, `skill_id`, `domain`, `description`, `chains_well_with`) -- same discovery the orchestrator already uses, excluding this file and the orchestrator itself. Match discovered skills against the `research_request` the same way the orchestrator's Step 3 does. Then `Read` `personas.md` to find each matched skill's voice: check **By Skill** first for an exact `skill_id` match, then fall back to **By Domain** using the skill's `domain` if no skill-specific entry exists. A matched skill with no entry in either table still joins the room, speaking under its own skill title rather than a named persona, until `personas.md` grows to cover it. The room's **Always Present** persona (`personas.md`) joins regardless of what the discovery step matched -- not tied to any domain, present in most sessions by design.

If `project_path` was given, do the Continuity reads (see below) alongside this step, before the room opens.

### 2. Voice Each Persona

Three ways to give each matched persona its voice, chosen by `dispatch_mode` (`input_schema`) -- though only two of them are actually distinct mechanisms; the third, `auto`, is a rule for choosing between the other two, not a mechanism of its own. Absent the field, or given `"session"`, this step runs exactly as it always has -- the paragraph below is unchanged from before `dispatch_mode` existed, and Proof of Concept #1 stays accurate to it.

**Session mode** (default). One mind behind every voice, each persona distinct in how it reasons and what it emphasizes, not just in name. Disagreement between disciplines is not resolved on the room's behalf -- when two personas' readings of the same evidence genuinely conflict, both stand, named, rather than one quietly winning. Personality, humor, and light rivalry between personas are welcome in the room precisely because they help the researcher track *who* holds *which* position -- but nothing about tone changes what a persona actually claims, and the finished, citable output (see Continuity) is never sourced from banter, only from what a persona substantively argued.

**Subagent mode** (`dispatch_mode: "subagent"`). Each matched persona other than Alberico (**Always Present**, `personas.md`) is voiced by a real, independent `Task` subagent (`general-purpose` type) instead of by the one orchestrating mind -- the same dispatch mechanism `skills/orchestrator/SKILL.md`'s Step 4 (Dispatch) already runs in production for its own, silent composition, reused here so a persona's *position* is a genuinely separate reasoning process rather than a simulated one. Alberico is never dispatched, in either mode -- he's facilitation and Continuity bookkeeping (see Continuity, below and Known Limitations), not a discipline position needing independent reasoning.

For each matched persona, build the subagent's prompt from two things kept deliberately separate -- `personas.md`'s own opening lines are explicit that personality and methodology must not mix, and that discipline holds here too:

1. **The discipline skill's own substance.** `Read` the matched skill's full `SKILL.md` and hand the subagent its actual `Procedure` and evidence -- the same methodological content the skill would produce for any other caller, unmodified.
2. **The persona voice, layered on top at dispatch time only.** The persona's name, icon, and trait from `personas.md`, plus an instruction for *how* to present the position in the room -- never written into the discipline skill's own file, never blended into its evidence.

Ask the subagent to actually apply the skill's procedure to the `research_request` and return a substantive position grounded in that skill's own evidence -- not a generic "here's what this skill does" summary. Run independent personas' subagents in parallel, the same discipline the orchestrator's Dispatch step already documents (don't reinvent it); dispatch sequentially only where one persona's input genuinely depends on another's already-returned output. This is rarer than the orchestrator's own pipeline-shaped compositions (where one skill routinely feeds the next), but not as rare as "basically never" in a discussion room -- a synthesis-flavored skill like `geography/regional-analysis`, whose whole job is integrating other geographic lenses, genuinely needs those other personas' outputs in hand to do that job for real; dispatching it in parallel alongside the skills it's meant to synthesize is a real latency-vs-correctness tradeoff, not a free win (see Proof of Concept #2).

Once every dispatched subagent has returned, weave the results into the room turn by turn -- ordering turns so a rebuttal lands after what it rebuts, adding the connective tissue real conversation has, letting one persona pick up a thread another dropped. That weaving/staging still happens in the single orchestrating mind, same as any conversation needs a narrator -- but the distinction that matters, and that a future reader should hold onto: in session mode, the *substance* of what a persona argues is invented by the one mind voicing everyone; in subagent mode, the substance comes verbatim-in-spirit from that persona's own subagent's independent output, and the orchestrating mind stages delivery without inventing, softening, or resolving what a subagent actually returned. This is why disagreement that survives subagent mode is a stronger signal than disagreement that survives session mode -- it means two independent reasoning processes reached different conclusions from the same evidence, not one mind narrating two viewpoints against itself.

**Auto mode** (`dispatch_mode: "auto"`). Not a third mechanism -- the same two above, session and subagent, with the orchestrating mind choosing between them per persona instead of committing to one for the whole room. The default lean is inline (session mode): fast and conversational, and right for most of what a room does. A persona is dispatched as a real subagent instead only when its position is plausibly going to genuinely diverge from another persona's on this specific `research_request` -- the disagreement itself is likely to be the useful thing the researcher needs to see -- or when the `research_request` explicitly asks a persona to dig in, analyze, or critique in a way one mind narrating every side tends to flatten. When it's a close call, stay inline; dispatch is the exception reached for, not the default.

The choice is made per persona, not once for the whole room: a request can reasonably dispatch two personas whose disagreement is the actual point (a fiscal-mechanism reading against a policy-framing reading, say) while voicing a third inline because its contribution is supportive or contextual rather than contested. It's also made per room, not per turn -- Tabula Rasa's rooms are shorter and more bounded than an open-ended party session, with no ongoing mid-conversation recalculation the way a long-running chat might need; the call is made once, at Discover the Room (Step 1), after personas are matched and before this step runs, and holds for the room from there. It is a judgment the orchestrating mind makes itself in that moment -- not a separate input the researcher configures persona by persona; `dispatch_mode: "auto"` is the only thing they set.

### 3. Continuity

Skipped entirely when `project_path` is absent, per `input_schema` -- the session runs single-shot with no read or write below. When present, two artifacts, both single-file-per-project (never split by day or session) and both located relative to `project_path`, self-contained -- no dependency on this repository's own `_bmad/` tooling, which does not ship with the plugin:

- **`{project_path}/.tabula-rasa/memoria.md`** -- hidden. The raw discussion log: what was discussed, and especially what was discussed but not yet decided (open threads). Grows across every Tabula Rasa session for the project. Internal/working file, not meant for the researcher to read directly, though nothing prevents it.
- **`{project_path}/seer_output/ata-do-projeto.md`** -- visible, under `seer_output/` (mirrors this repo's own `_bmad-output/` convention, one layer up, for the same kind of tool-owned-but-legible output). The decisions record: what got closed, adopted methodology, resolved threads -- a living document for the *project*, reorganized as decisions evolve, not append-only the way `memoria.md` is.

**Read (part of Discover the Room, above).** Each file independently, only if it exists; neither existing is not an error, the session simply opens cold on that front. The two files are read differently, because they aren't the same kind of artifact: `ata-do-projeto.md` is a curated, reorganized living document (see below) that stays a reasonable size by construction, so it is always `Read` directly -- its content shapes how the room opens, and a decision already closed there is not relitigated as if it were new. `memoria.md` is the unbounded append-only log, so how it's read is a judgment call, not a fixed rule: on a first session, or whenever the file is absent or still trivially short, just `Read` it directly -- dispatching a subagent for a near-empty file is wasted overhead. Once it's accrued substantial content across sessions, dispatch a `Task` subagent (`general-purpose` type) to read the full file and return a compact, distilled brief -- open threads and whatever color is actually relevant, not a recap or a transcript -- the same discipline `.claude/skills/bmad-party-mode/references/party-memory.md`'s "read it on entry -- distill, don't dump" describes, now using the tool this skill actually has rather than reading the raw log in full every time. Either way, the open threads are raised by Alberico (**Always Present**, `personas.md`) organically, near the start, worked into how he opens the room in character -- never a recited log dump, never "I loaded a file" breaking the fourth wall.

**Write, `ata-do-projeto.md` -- during the session, as decisions close.** The moment something genuinely becomes a closed decision -- resolved thread, adopted methodology, a position the room settled on -- write it then, not held for Wrapping Up; the session could end abruptly before wrap-up is reached. Use `Edit` on the specific existing section a decision updates or supersedes (this is a living document); `Write` a new section only for a decision with no existing home. Constitution Principle 6 applies here exactly as `docs/skill-contract.md`'s Project Context section already applies it to `metodologia.md`: if `ata-do-projeto.md`'s existing content conflicts with what a discipline skill in the room would normally argue, the room flags the tension to the researcher rather than silently overriding either the skill's default or the ata's prior decision.

**Write, `memoria.md` -- once, at Wrapping Up.** Unlike the ata, this is reasonably composed a single time, at Wrapping Up (below) -- its session block is a summary of the whole session's open threads as they stand at the end, so composing it earlier would mean redoing it. `Read` the existing file if present, then `Write` the full file back with the new session's block appended at the end under a dated heading (e.g. `## Sessão {date}`) -- prior content is never overwritten or discarded, only added to. If the file doesn't exist yet, this `Write` creates it under `.tabula-rasa/`.

Both writes are silent -- the room never says "noting this" or "I'll remember this," same discipline as the `party-memory.md` reference above.

**Relationship to `metodologia.md` is deliberately unresolved.** `metodologia.md` (`docs/skill-contract.md`, Project Context) is narrower -- which methodology was adopted -- and read by any skill in Seer, not just this one. `ata-do-projeto.md` is broader -- any project decision -- and is Tabula-Rasa-specific. This version does not merge them, cross-reference them, or make one a subset of the other; a future increment may need to reconcile the two, but that is an open question, not something decided here.

### 4. Wrapping Up

When the researcher signals the session is done, the **Always Present** persona reads back the open threads plainly -- what got settled, what didn't -- and the room returns to normal skill behavior. This is also when `memoria.md`'s session block gets written, if Continuity is active (see above) -- the same open threads just read back, composed into the dated entry. No transcript of the room's banter is ever the deliverable; if the researcher wants a written output from the discussion, that gets composed the same way any skill's `output_schema` would, sourced from what was substantively argued.

## Example

**Input:** `research_request`: "Alberico e Serafim, me ajudem a pensar se dá pra tratar o recorte territorial da minha pesquisa como Geografia pura ou se isso já vira uma questão de política educacional."

**Room (shape, not full transcript):** Alberico opens from what the territorial data actually supports -- and where it stops supporting a claim. Serafim pushes back that the moment funding or curriculum enters the picture, it stops being descriptive Geography and starts being a policy question neither of them can settle alone -- and says so directly, not as a footnote. The disagreement is left standing, named, with both positions attributed, and the researcher is asked which framing she actually needs to answer next.

## Proof of Concept

**#1.** Run on the same real request as the orchestrator's Cowork proof of concept #2 (`skills/orchestrator/SKILL.md`), deliberately reused so the two composition modes are comparable on identical input: *"Analise como a queda da taxa de natalidade no Nordeste brasileiro pode afetar o planejamento educacional regional nos próximos 10 anos."*

Discovery matched `geography/human-geography`, `geography/economic-geography`, and `education/educational-policy` against the request; `personas.md` resolved both geography skills to Alberico and the education skill to Serafim. The room ran for real, grounded in each matched skill's own evidence base, not improvised:

- Alberico opened from `economic-geography`'s scale-mismatch discipline -- decline in natural growth is unlikely to be spatially uniform across the Northeast, and FUNDEB's enrollment-linked funding formula means municipalities with a weaker tax base absorb an enrollment decline harder than better-resourced ones, independent of the raw regional percentage. He argued mapping *where* the decline concentrates has to come before any regional plan.
- Serafim, from `educational-policy`, didn't contest the map -- he contested where it puts the leverage. Ball's finding (policy is reshaped by whoever implements it, and effects stratify even under formally universal rules) means the same demographic map produces different real outcomes depending on municipal incentives: a district dependent on enrollment-linked funding has a structural reason to resist consolidating schools even where demographically justified, and a poorer municipality without a well-resourced district's slack is more likely to lose a school outright than gain smaller class sizes from the same decline.
- Neither position resolved the other's, deliberately -- the room closed by handing the researcher an explicit fork (map the concentration first, or take the concentration as given and design the policy response first) rather than picking one, matching the same tension-preserving discipline `skills/orchestrator/SKILL.md` already applies to composition.

**Real finding, not anticipated when this skill was written:** `personas.md` maps persona-per-*domain*, not persona-per-*skill*. Here two geography skills (`human-geography`, `economic-geography`) both matched, and both were voiced as Alberico -- workable because a single geographer plausibly holds both lenses at once and they agreed in spirit. This would not hold if two same-domain skills genuinely disagreed with *each other* rather than with a different domain's persona -- the room has no mechanism yet to voice that as two distinct positions. Not fixed now; recorded below as an open design question for whenever it actually happens in a real session, not solved speculatively.

**#2.** `dispatch_mode: "subagent"` validated end to end, on the same request as #1 above (deliberately reused again, so all three -- session mode, subagent mode, and the orchestrator's own silent composition -- are comparable on identical input): *"Analise como a queda da taxa de natalidade no Nordeste brasileiro pode afetar o planejamento educacional regional nos próximos 10 anos."* `project_path` omitted, to isolate the dispatch mechanism from Continuity.

**Environment note, stated plainly:** this ran via a direct `claude -p --dangerously-skip-permissions` invocation reading this file's own working-tree source, not through the installed Cowork plugin -- the installed plugin's marketplace source is GitHub, refreshed by `claude plugin marketplace update`, and confirmed (by grepping the post-reinstall plugin cache for `dispatch_mode`) not to pick up this repo's uncommitted local edits. That's an environment constraint of validating an unmerged change, not a property of the feature -- the dispatch mechanism exercised (`Task` subagents, `general-purpose` type) is the same regardless of which surface loaded the `SKILL.md`.

Discovery matched the same three skills as #1: `geography/human-geography` (Maria), `geography/economic-geography` (Catarina), `education/educational-policy` (Serafim); `geography/regional-analysis` was correctly excluded, for the same reason the orchestrator's own Cowork proof of concept excluded it on this identical request (one region, two lenses, not a multi-region synthesis). Each persona was dispatched as a real, independent `Task` subagent -- given that skill's own real `Procedure`/evidence (read from its `SKILL.md`) plus the persona's voice instruction layered on top, the two kept separate exactly as `personas.md`'s opening lines require. Each subagent was asked to apply its skill's procedure to the request and return a substantive position, not a summary -- and did:

- **Maria** entered through spatial heterogeneity in the demographic pattern: the regional TFT collapse (4.5 -> under 1.8) hides that semiárido interior municipalities are still above 2.5 and converging late, and named a live opportunity window (the Bolsa Família cohort now reaching Ensino Médio) that a Fundamental-only reading of the decline would miss entirely.
- **Catarina** entered through the FUNDEB fiscal mechanism, not the demographic pattern -- enrollment-linked funding means the real risk is fiscal collapse in FUNDEB-dependent municipios, ahead of the enrollment decline itself, and named a specific triple-vulnerability profile (FUNDEB dependence + youth outmigration + 2000s-era overcapacity). She held a genuine, stated uncertainty about whether FUNDEB's 2020 reform (EC 108/2020, VAAR) buffers this.
- **Serafim** entered through Ball's problem-definition contest -- a deficit framing structurally beats an opportunity framing because municipal secretaries answer to four-year electoral horizons, not long-run quality gains -- and independently sharpened Catarina's open VAAR question into a specific claim: it addresses income floors, not institutional capacity, which are two different deficits.

**Real tension that survived the weave, not smoothed over:** Maria's claimed Ensino Médio Técnico opportunity window met a direct structural counter from Catarina -- the municipalities that most need that window are the ones least able to open it, so the same opportunity reproduces existing inequality rather than correcting it. Neither position folded into the other in the woven transcript; both stand, attributed, exactly as Voice Each Persona requires in either mode.

**What this validates, and what it doesn't.** It confirms the mechanism runs for real: three genuinely separate `Task` subagents, each reasoning from a different entry point into the same evidence base (spatial pattern, fiscal mechanism, policy-framing contest) rather than one mind covering all three angles, producing disagreement (the VAAR mechanism claim, the Ensino Médio window's real reach) that reads as two independent passes converging on the same object rather than one pass narrating two views of it. It does not include independent, external timing instrumentation confirming the three subagents actually executed concurrently rather than one after another -- parallelism here rests on the same basis Proof of Concept #1's "shape, not full transcript" framing already accepted: the orchestrating process's own account of what it did, not an outside measurement.

**Independent re-run, same request, different environment.** A second, separately-run `claude -p` invocation -- through a locally-served plugin install rather than a direct file read, avoiding the first run's `--dangerously-skip-permissions` workaround -- matched a broader set of six personas on the identical request (Maria, Catarina, Altamira, Serafim, Asdrubal, Ludovico), producing an even richer independent-disagreement result: Catarina (fiscal-structural) vs. Ludovico (Weberian interpretive) on whether budget constraint mechanically forces a reframing of the problem, and Serafim (corrigible policy design) vs. Asdrubal (Kingdon-framework political feasibility) on whether the incentive problem is fixable in a ten-year horizon -- both genuine, neither smoothed over. It also surfaced a real limitation this procedure's text now names above: `regional-analysis` (Altamira) was dispatched in parallel with the geography/education personas it would, in a fuller treatment, want to synthesize -- so she reasoned from her own domain knowledge of the Northeast rather than from Maria's and Catarina's actual dispatched outputs, a real latency-vs-correctness tradeoff the orchestrating process itself flagged rather than hid.

**#3.** Continuity validated end to end with two real, separate `claude -p` invocations against the same `project_path` (a scratch test folder), each a full Read/Write/Task-dispatch session, not a simulation.

- **Session 1** (`research_request`: whether a rural school-dropout study should be framed as Geography, acesso físico, or Educação, política de permanência -- deliberately left open): Ubaldo and Serafim argued it in character, converged that the right framing depends on which rural profile the researcher is actually studying, and the session closed with the tension explicitly unresolved. Continuity wrote both files for the first time: `.tabula-rasa/memoria.md` (a dated `## Sessão {date}` block, the open thread stated plainly) and `seer_output/ata-do-projeto.md` (an "Em Aberto" section for the unresolved framing question, "Decisões Fechadas" left empty since nothing had closed).
- **Session 2**, same `project_path`, a deliberately thin prompt ("voltei, me ajudem a avançar") carrying no restated context: Alberico opened *in character* directly from the unresolved framing question -- without narrating that a file had been loaded -- and the room advanced the thread for real (Ubaldo proposed a three-profile typology of rural contexts, each implying a different literature base). `memoria.md` got a second dated block appended below the first, unmodified. `ata-do-projeto.md`'s existing "Em Aberto" section was `Edit`-updated in place with the new typology, not duplicated as a new section -- confirming the living-document behavior the Procedure specifies, distinct from `memoria.md`'s pure append.

This is the strongest evidence yet that Continuity behaves as designed rather than merely as written: real state survived a genuinely separate process invocation, the fourth wall held, and the two files kept their distinct write disciplines (append vs. in-place edit) under an actual second session, not just a read of the Procedure text.

## Known Limitations

- **No hardcoded threshold for "substantial enough to distill."** `memoria.md`'s direct-read-vs-dispatch choice (see Continuity above) is left to the model's judgment each session rather than a precise line-count or token cutoff -- deliberately, matching how the rest of this skill avoids hardcoding thresholds elsewhere, but it does mean two sessions could judge the same file differently.
- **Auto mode's per-persona dispatch call has no hard rule either, and that's the same deliberate choice as the bullet above, applied to a different decision.** `dispatch_mode: "auto"` (Voice Each Persona, Auto mode) is made once at Discover the Room, per persona, on the orchestrating mind's read of whether that persona's position is likely to genuinely diverge from another's -- not a line-count, a keyword list, or any other hardcoded trigger. Two sessions matching the same personas against a similar `research_request` could reasonably land on different calls, one dispatching a persona the other voices inline, without either being wrong. Auto mode doesn't remove the latency/cost tradeoff the "Subagent mode's independence is real" bullet below names -- it just decides, room by room and persona by persona, when that tradeoff is worth paying instead of committing to one answer for the whole session.
- **`ata-do-projeto.md` and `metodologia.md` are two distinct artifacts, deliberately kept separate.** The former is Tabula-Rasa-specific, internal project tracking (decisions, resolved threads) not meant to feed the researcher's actual publication. The latter is skill-agnostic, covers only adopted methodology, and is written with an eye toward being incorporated into the final published work itself (a thesis, paper, or book's own Methodology section) -- a real audience difference, not an oversight, and why they are not merged or cross-referenced.
- **Subagent mode's independence is real, and it costs real latency and money.** `dispatch_mode: "subagent"` (Voice Each Persona) gives each persona its own `Task` subagent, genuinely reasoning through its discipline skill's procedure rather than being simulated by the one orchestrating mind -- validated end to end in Proof of Concept #2, above. That independence isn't free: each dispatched persona is a real reasoning pass, so a multi-persona subagent-mode room is slower and costs more than the same room in session mode. It's worth that cost specifically when independent reasoning is likely to change the outcome -- a single-persona room, or a request the matched personas are unlikely to actually disagree on, gets little benefit from dispatch, and session mode is the better default there. This is a judgment call left to whoever invokes the skill (or the researcher, if `dispatch_mode` is exposed to them) -- this skill does not silently pick a mode on their behalf in either direction.
- **Dispatched personas don't see Continuity, by design, and that's a real gap.** A persona's subagent in subagent mode is built only from its discipline skill's procedure and its persona voice (Voice Each Persona) -- never from `memoria.md`'s open threads or `ata-do-projeto.md`'s closed decisions, even when `project_path` is given and Continuity is active. This keeps the dispatch mechanism simple and self-contained rather than growing every persona's prompt with project state it may not need, but the cost is real: a dispatched subagent can independently land on a position that conflicts with a decision `ata-do-projeto.md` already closed, having never seen it. The orchestrating mind -- which does read Continuity, in Discover the Room -- is responsible for catching that at weave time and flagging the tension to the researcher, the same Constitution Principle 6 discipline the Continuity section already applies to `ata-do-projeto.md` conflicts; it must not silently edit what the subagent returned to make it agree. A future increment may pass a distilled Continuity brief into dispatched prompts if this proves to lose real decisions in practice -- not done now, and not attempted speculatively.
- **`personas.md` coverage lags the skill roster.** A newly contributed discipline skill joins the room under its own title, not a named persona, until `personas.md` is updated -- this is a deliberate default, not a bug to route around.
- **Persona granularity is per-domain by default, with a per-skill override tier (`personas.md`, By Skill) for domains where it's actually needed.** Geography uses the override tier today (nine skills split across eight distinct voices, addressing the exact gap Proof of Concept #1 surfaced); every other domain still shares one persona across all its skills. This is deliberate, not a gap to close everywhere -- adding a By Skill override should stay reserved for domains with a real, demonstrated risk of same-domain disagreement, not become the default pattern.
