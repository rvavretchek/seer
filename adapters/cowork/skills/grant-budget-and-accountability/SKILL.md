# Grant Budget and Accountability (CAPES, CNPq, FAPESP)

## What This Skill Does

Helps a Brazilian researcher manage the financial side of a funded research project across three distinct phases: building the budget (orçamento) for a project or anteprojeto before submission; tracking spend against budget categories (rubricas) while the funded project is running; and preparing the closeout financial report (prestação de contas) a funding agency requires at the end. It covers the three major Brazilian research-funding agencies -- CAPES, CNPq, and FAPESP -- but is deliberately built around the general structural patterns and compliance principles that recur across most editais (calls for proposals) from each agency, not a claim to be THE rule for any specific one. Grant-compliance rules for these agencies are not one canonical document each: they vary by edital and modalidade within each agency, and change over time (CAPES replaced a 13-year-old portaria in 2026; CNPq and FAPESP each maintain their own separately-versioned norms). Treating a general pattern as if it were a specific edital's binding rule would be actively harmful to a researcher preparing a real submission or closeout report -- this skill is built to be explicit, at every step, about where its grounding is a solid general default and where the researcher must check their own edital, Termo de Outorga, or instrução normativa before relying on it.

This is a document-formatting and compliance-checking skill for financial content, distinct from the scientific/methodological content of a proposal -- it does not evaluate whether a project's aims are sound, only whether its budget or financial report follows the structural and documentary patterns funding agencies typically require.

## Evidence Foundation

All three agencies distribute public or quasi-public research funds and, unlike ABNT's small number of stable NBR standards, none of them publishes one single canonical document that fully specifies "the" budget and accountability rules -- each maintains a general normative framework (a manual, a set of resolutions, or a portaria) that individual editais then narrow, extend, or override. This skill's evidence comes from that general framework, confirmed per agency as follows -- and the edital-variation caveat below applies to every rubrica category, percentage, and deadline mentioned anywhere in this skill, not just here.

**Structural root.** The custeio (day-to-day operating costs) vs. capital (durable goods, useful life beyond the project) split that recurs across all three agencies' rubrica taxonomies traces back to Lei nº 4.320/1964, Brazil's general public-finance law distinguishing despesas correntes from despesas de capital. This is shared legal infrastructure, not something CAPES, CNPq, or FAPESP each invented independently -- which is part of why the split is genuinely a safe general default even though each agency's specific item lists differ.

**CNPq.** The current official CNPq prestação de contas page (fetched directly) states a 60-day deadline after the end of a grant's vigência. Secondary sources (multiple independent Brazilian university research-office guidance pages, cross-checked against each other) converge on RN-017/2011 (Auxílios Individuais, the APQ modality most relevant to an individual researcher's project grant), RN-008/2008 (basis for returning unused/unjustified balances), Portaria CNPq nº 914/2022 (reported as establishing the current Manual de Prestação de Contas), and Decreto nº 9.283/2018 (the Marco Legal de CT&I decree cited as CNPq's legal basis for requiring accountability). A "Manual de Utilização de Recursos Financeiros e Elaboração de Prestação de Contas" is referenced under RN-313/2022 in CNPq's own Portal Memória index, but this skill could not independently confirm that number against one single authoritative current index -- CNPq does not appear to publish a single consolidated "which RNs are in force now" list the way ABNT's standards catalog does, so multiple RN numbers reported here should be treated as corroborated-but-not-primary-verified.

**FAPESP.** The current primary source (fapesp.br/normaspc, fetched directly) confirms "Normas para Uso de Recursos e Prestação de Contas de Auxílios e Bolsas," effective from 16 May 2021, replacing a version in force 02 August 2011 - 15 May 2021. It establishes despesas de capital (material permanente) vs. despesas de custeio (material de consumo, serviços de terceiros, despesas de transporte, diárias/manutenção), lists commonly non-financeable items, and -- notably -- states the prestação de contas deadline is set case-by-case in each grant's own Termo de Outorga rather than a single fixed day-count. This is a real structural difference from CNPq's and CAPES's fixed deadlines, confirmed from FAPESP's own page, not a gap in this skill's research.

**CAPES.** Portaria CAPES nº 37, de 26 de janeiro de 2026, fully revokes Portaria CAPES nº 59, de 14 de maio de 2013 -- the regulation that had governed AUXPE (Auxílio Financeiro a Projeto Educacional ou de Pesquisa) and the SIPREC online accountability system for over a decade. This is confirmed via legislative-index pages (abmes.org.br, semesp.org.br) and a secondary summary (crub.org.br), and took effect 28 April 2026 (90 days after publication, which is why it is already in force as of this skill's writing but was not the applicable rule for a project closed out before that date). Reported concrete changes: inter-category budget reallocation (remanejamento) up to 20% of the total transferred amount no longer requires notifying CAPES; purchase invoices are now submitted automatically through an updated SIPREC (relaunched 2025); and the Cartão Pesquisador (a Banco do Brasil-issued card) is now formally recognized for the grant holder and other project participants. A 60-day-after-vigência SIPREC deadline was reported by secondary sources -- matching CNPq's figure -- but this skill could not independently confirm it against the new portaria's own text directly.

Because grant rules turn over on no predictable public schedule (CAPES went 13 years between Portaria 59/2013 and Portaria 37/2026, then changed meaningfully), and because every edital can narrow or override its parent agency's general framework, `evidence_strength` here is rated **moderate** -- the same rating and for a closely related reason as `core/academic-formatting-abnt`, but with an additional, larger layer of risk that ABNT does not carry: even a fully-current, fully-verified agency-level rule can still be superseded by the specific edital or Termo de Outorga governing an individual grant.

## Procedure

Treat budget formation, execution tracking, and prestação de contas as three separate passes -- they happen at different points in a project's life and use different inputs, though a well-run project's tracking phase should make the closeout phase mostly a matter of compiling what was already tracked correctly.

1. **Confirm agency, edital/modalidade, and phase before doing anything else.** State explicitly which of CAPES, CNPq, or FAPESP applies (a different agency's rules do not transfer), and ask for the specific `edital_or_modalidade` if not supplied -- without it, this skill can only offer the agency-level general pattern, which is a starting point, not a finished budget or report. If `edital_or_modalidade` is genuinely unavailable, say so and proceed on the general pattern with `verification_flags` populated accordingly, rather than silently treating the general pattern as edital-specific.

2. **Phase 1 -- Budget formation (orçamento).** Build the budget as a set of rubrica line items grouped into the custeio/capital split (or the closer agency-specific variant -- FAPESP's material permanente/material de consumo/serviços de terceiros/despesas de transporte/diárias breakdown, or CNPq's comparable categories under its own manual). For each line item: state the rubrica category, the amount, and a one-line justification tying it to the project's actual planned activities (fieldwork, equipment, dissemination). Flag any item that resembles a commonly-restricted category across the sources this skill drew on -- personnel/payroll-style salaries, administrative overhead, non-economy-class air travel, insurance on movable assets, family-member contracting, course/tuition fees -- as needing edital-specific confirmation rather than silently including or excluding it. Never state a specific percentage cap, a specific rubrica ceiling, or a specific currency figure (e.g. a maximum daily rate for diárias, a bolsa's monthly value) as current unless it was directly confirmed in this skill's own research; funding-agency value tables (tabelas de valores de bolsas, diária rates) are revised on their own schedule and go stale fast.

3. **Phase 2 -- Execution tracking (during the funded period).** Once a project is funded, track actual spend against the planned rubrica breakdown from Phase 1, flagging: (a) spend that has drifted into a different rubrica category than planned, since inter-category reallocation is often restricted or capped (CAPES's 2026 rule permits up to 20% without notification, as one concrete confirmed example -- but this is CAPES-specific and effective only from 28 April 2026 forward, not a number to reuse for CNPq or FAPESP without separate confirmation); (b) documentation gaps -- CNPq and CAPES both structure this around each expense needing a nota fiscal or recibo tied to the beneficiary's CPF and process number, a pattern that recurs across the sources this skill drew on even where exact retention periods vary; (c) approaching the reported deadline pattern for the agency in question (60 days after vigência ends, for CNPq and reportedly for CAPES; case-by-case per the Termo de Outorga, for FAPESP).

4. **Phase 3 -- Prestação de contas (closeout).** Compile the final financial report: total spend by rubrica against the approved budget, supporting documentation (notas fiscais, recibos, comprovantes), and the return of any unused/unjustified balance to the agency -- a pattern found across all three agencies' general frameworks, though the precise mechanism and deadline vary. State the deadline pattern found for the stated agency in `deadline_notes`, explicitly marked as the general default rather than a confirmed figure for this specific grant, and recommend the researcher confirm the exact deadline against their own Termo de Outorga or edital before relying on it -- a missed prestação de contas deadline can result in the researcher being marked inadimplente with the agency, which can block future proposals, a consequence too serious for this skill to leave ambiguous.

5. **Surface institutional variation explicitly, never silently overrule it.** When `institutional_rules` is supplied and conflicts with this skill's agency-level default output, state the tension and defer to the institutional rule (Constitution, Principle 6) -- Brazilian universities and their fundações de apoio (e.g. FUSP, FUNCAMP, FINATEC) commonly add requirements on top of the bare agency norms (an internal approval workflow, a stricter documentation standard, a different reimbursement process), and a researcher's actual working requirement is whatever their institution enforces, not this skill's generic default.

6. **Compose with `core/academic-writing` for budget-justification prose.** This skill produces the rubrica breakdown and compliance flags; the narrative justificativa text a proposal often requires (explaining why each major budget item is needed) is a drafting task better handled by `core/academic-writing`, which this skill's output can feed as an evidence trail.

## Example

**Input:** `financial_task`: "budget formation" -- a researcher is drafting the orçamento section of a small field-research anteprojeto (roughly R$ 30,000) for interviews and site visits in a human-geography study. `funding_agency`: "FAPESP". `edital_or_modalidade`: "Auxílio Regular à Pesquisa" (not a specific numbered edital -- the researcher hasn't picked one yet).

**Output (abridged):**

**rubrica_breakdown:**

| Rubrica | Category | Item | Amount (R$) | Note |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Despesas de transporte | Custeio | Passagens rodoviárias, 3 field trips | 2,400 | Standard custeio item; economy-class/standard fare per FAPESP's confirmed non-financeable list for airfare above economy class -- not applicable here since these are road trips, but flag if any leg later becomes air travel. |
| Diárias e manutenção | Custeio | Diárias for 12 field days | 3,600 | Well-corroborated rubrica category; the specific daily rate table is a FAPESP-published value that changes on its own schedule -- **verification_flags**: do not treat any specific R$/day figure as current without checking FAPESP's own diária table at submission time. |
| Material de consumo | Custeio | Recording media, printed consent forms, transcription supplies | 1,800 | Standard custeio item, well-corroborated. |
| Serviços de terceiros | Custeio | Freelance interview transcription service | 4,000 | Standard custeio item -- **compliance_flags**: FAPESP's confirmed norms restrict "serviços de terceiros" financing to technical/eventual (non-continuous) services; confirm this transcription contract is structured as a one-off service, not something resembling an ongoing employment relationship, before finalizing. |
| Material permanente | Capital | One digital voice recorder | 900 | Standard capital item -- durable good, useful life beyond the project. |

**deadline_notes:** FAPESP's prestação de contas deadline is set in the grant's own Termo de Outorga rather than a fixed day-count agency-wide (confirmed from FAPESP's current norms page) -- this cannot be stated as a specific date until the Auxílio is actually granted and the Termo issued.

**verification_flags:**
- The specific edital number for this Auxílio Regular à Pesquisa call was not supplied -- confirm the current call's own text doesn't narrow or add to any rubrica above before submitting.
- Diária daily-rate figures and any FAPESP percentage cap on how much of the total budget diárias may represent were not independently confirmed by this skill and must be checked against FAPESP's current published tables.
- Whether this specific project qualifies as fieldwork under FAPESP's travel-expense exception (travel is otherwise commonly restricted) should be confirmed explicitly in the proposal's justificativa, not assumed from this general pattern.

## Known Limitations

- **Edital and modalidade variation is the primary, structural limitation of this skill -- not a minor caveat.** CAPES, CNPq, and FAPESP each run dozens of active editais at any time, and any of them can narrow, extend, or override its parent agency's general rubrica list, percentage caps, or deadlines. This skill's `rubrica_breakdown`, `deadline_notes`, and `compliance_flags` are general defaults built from each agency's own overarching norms -- never a substitute for reading the specific edital and Termo de Outorga governing a real grant. A researcher who finalizes a real budget or prestação de contas report using only this skill's output, without checking their own edital text, is taking on real financial and administrative risk.
- **Specific rubrica limits, percentages, and deadlines this skill could not verify were flagged, not invented.** The one concrete percentage this skill can state with confidence is CAPES's 2026 rule permitting up to 20% inter-category reallocation without notification -- and even that applies only under Portaria 37/2026 (effective 28 April 2026 forward) and only to CAPES AUXPE, not to CNPq or FAPESP. No comparable CNPq or FAPESP percentage cap was found and confirmed; none should be assumed to exist or to match CAPES's figure.
- **Currency figures decay fast and are deliberately absent from this skill.** Bolsa monthly values, diária daily rates, and specific R$ ceilings are published by each agency in their own tables, revised on their own schedule (often annually, sometimes more often) -- this skill states rubrica categories and structural patterns, never a specific current R$ figure, because any such figure would be stale by the time this skill is read.
- **This skill's grounding on some CNPq document numbers (notably RN-313/2022) is corroborated-but-not-primary-verified.** Unlike ABNT's small number of stable NBR standards, CNPq does not appear to publish one single consolidated "currently in force" index of its Resoluções Normativas the way ABNT's catalog works -- the RN numbers cited in Evidence Foundation came from multiple independent secondary sources agreeing with each other, not from directly reading a canonical current text. Treat them as a strong starting point for further verification, not a confirmed citation.
- **Institutional variation is real and takes precedence.** A researcher's own university and its fundação de apoio (e.g. FUSP, FUNCAMP, FINATEC, or equivalents) commonly layer additional approval workflows, documentation standards, or reimbursement processes on top of the bare funding-agency norms. This skill's output is the agency-level default, not a guarantee of matching a specific institution's actual working requirements -- always defer to the researcher's own institutional rules when supplied, per the Procedure above.
- **Enforcement strictness varies and this skill cannot predict it.** How tightly a given program officer, agency, or edital actually audits a prestação de contas -- versus how strict the written rule technically is -- varies in practice and isn't something this skill can observe or forecast; treat its compliance flags as "worth confirming," not as a guarantee of what will or won't be questioned.
- **This is the first skill in the `research-finance` domain; no sibling skills exist yet.** Unlike ABNT (a country-specific regulatory standard carved out from the language/locale-variant pattern per `docs/skill-contract.md`), this skill is not a locale variant either -- it is a multi-agency regulatory-compliance skill in a new domain. Future `research-finance` skills (e.g. covering a specific agency in more depth, or state-level foundations like FAPERJ/FAPEMIG) should be named as their own skills within this domain, not folded into this one as hidden conditional logic.
- **Not a substitute for a human final check, especially before a real submission or closeout.** Given the direct financial and administrative consequences of an error (a rejected budget line, a delayed or failed prestação de contas leaving the researcher inadimplente with the agency), this skill's output should be treated as a strong draft/checklist pass -- a human (the researcher, their institution's pró-reitoria de pesquisa, or a contador familiar with the specific agency) should do the final verification pass, per Constitution Principle 6.
- **Does not cover the scientific or methodological content of a proposal.** This skill handles budget and accountability documents only; compose with `core/academic-writing` for the narrative sections of a proposal, and with domain-specific skills (e.g. under `geography/`, `sociology/`) for the research design itself.
